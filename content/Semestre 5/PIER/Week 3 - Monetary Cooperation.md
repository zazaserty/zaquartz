### Gold standard mentality
#### Beliefs among elites
- Gold Standard = stability, civilization, morality. Abandoning gold = chaos and social disorder.
- Adjustment through deflation, not devaluation: raise interest rates, cut wages, no monetary/fiscal stimulus
#### Result
Procyclical policy turned 1929 recession into a depression
### Keynesian critique
- Observation: persistent unemployment, commercial competition in 1920s/30s.
- Interpretation: failure of internal adjustment; harmful use of tariffs as alternative.
- Conclusion: Gold standard as “barbaric relic”; prioritize internal over external stability; use monetary flexibility.

### Monetarism and RBC
- Monetarism: money supply as key driver of inflation; temporary real effect of monetary stimulus, with risk of stagflation.
- ‘Real Business Cycles’: technology drives fluctuations in growth; ineffective monetary policy due to rational expectations.
- Conclusions: limited policy interventions; no ‘fine tuning’; focus on price stability.

##### Solution: institutions/rules
- CB Independence: delegation of monetary policy to a ‘conservative’ central banker; prices stability as primary objective.
- Fixed Rates: peg currency to country with high monetary credibility; costly commitment to monetary stability; easily observable.
### Conclusion
- Democracy requires (some) monetary flexibility.
- Shift away from ‘fine tuning’ towards price stability focus.
- Institutions solve monetary cooperation and credibility problems.
